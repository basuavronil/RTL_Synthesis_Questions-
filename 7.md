### Q: Can you mix blocking and non-blocking assignments to the same variable?

**A:** It is **bad practice**, and many tools will throw an error or warning. 

#### Why It Is Bad Practice
1. **Simulation vs. Synthesis Mismatch:** Blocking assignments (`=`) update variables immediately, while non-blocking assignments (`<=`) queue updates for the end of the time step. Mixing them causes simulation behavior to diverge from actual synthesized hardware.
2. **Race Conditions:** It introduces unpredictable race conditions where the final value depends heavily on execution order rather than intended logic.
3. **Ambiguous Hardware Inference:** Synthesis tools rely on consistent patterns (blocking for combinational logic, non-blocking for sequential flip-flops). Mixing them confuses the compiler, often resulting in unintended latches or broken logic.

#### Standard Rules to Follow
* Never mix blocking and non-blocking assignments on the same variable.
* Use **non-blocking (`<=`)** exclusively for sequential logic (`always @(posedge clk)`).
* Use **blocking (`=`)** exclusively for combinational logic (`always @(*)`).
