# Verilog & SystemVerilog RTL Design FAQ

## FAQ: Why are latches problematic in ASIC/FPGA flows?

### Why are latches problematic in ASIC/FPGA flows?
They are hard for STA (time borrowing), hard for DFT/scan, and prone to glitches.

---

## Detailed Breakdown of Latch Issues in Silicon Design

### 1. Hard for Static Timing Analysis (STA)
* **Time Borrowing:** Unlike edge-triggered flip-flops that capture data strictly on a clock edge, latches are transparent while their enable signal is active. This allows logic stages to "borrow time" from adjacent cycles, making timing closure complex, unpredictable, and prone to race conditions if not meticulously constrained.

### 2. Hard for DFT and Scan Testing
* **Transparent Windows:** Standard Automatic Test Pattern Generation (ATPG) relies heavily on predictable, edge-triggered scan chains (flip-flops) to shift test vectors in and out. Because latches remain transparent when enabled, they break standard scan chain assumptions and complicate fault coverage and testability.

### 3. Prone to Glitches
* **Level-Sensitive Vulnerability:** Since latches pass data directly through whenever the enable input is active, any electrical noise, combinational hazards, or voltage glitches on the enable line will propagate instantly to the output, corrupting downstream logic state and causing functional failures.
